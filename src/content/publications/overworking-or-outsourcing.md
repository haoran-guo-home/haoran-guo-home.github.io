abstract: |
  Effective overtime control is essential for both operational sustainability and workforce well-being under workload fluctuations and uncertainty. In operational settings such as last-mile delivery, there are often external options to outsource workload at a higher cost, introducing a key trade-off between overworking and the cost of outsourcing. To navigate this trade-off under uncertainty, the two-step predict-then-optimize (PTO) paradigm faces a fundamental misalignment: standard prediction models are trained with statistical objectives that disregard downstream decision outcomes, leading to excessive overworking and compromised employee well-being. To address this challenge, we formulate this problem as a contextual stochastic optimization (CSO) and propose an integrated prediction-optimization (IPO) framework. Leveraging the problem structure, we derive performance-guaranteed point predictions for this CSO problem despite the nonlinear objective. Theoretically, we construct a decision-aware predictor through geometric analysis of the α-superlevel set and establish its excess decision risk bound. Guided by theoretical insights on the selection of probability mass α, we develop two efficient algorithms for practical implementation along with consistency results. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms standard PTO methods. Challenging a prevalent assumption, we find that simple, decision-focused predictions can outperform complex, decision-blind neural networks in decision quality. A case study in urban last-mile delivery is conducted to validate the practical relevance of our model and distill managerial implications. Results reveal that our approach has a growing advantage as the overtime penalty increases, and that overtime is often driven by a small subset of tasks, which should be identified in a decision-aware manner.
